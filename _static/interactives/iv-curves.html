How this model was built

The model

All curves come from the Hodgkin–Huxley model of the squid giant axon (Hodgkin and Huxley, 1952, J Physiol 117:500–544). The membrane carries three currents, each a conductance times a driving force:

I_Na = ḡ_Na · m³ · h · (V − E_Na) I_K = ḡ_K · n⁴ · (V − E_K) I_L = g_L · (V − E_L)

The gating variables m (Na⁺ activation), h (Na⁺ inactivation), and n (K⁺ activation) each range from 0 to 1 and change over time according to

dx/dt = α_x(V) · (1 − x) − β_x(V) · x

Currents use the modern convention: inward current is negative, and voltage is the actual membrane potential, with rest at about −65 mV. Hodgkin and Huxley measured voltage relative to rest and used the opposite sign, so their rate equations have been shifted to this convention.

Simulated voltage-clamp I-V curves

Each I-V curve simulates a family of voltage-clamp steps from the −65 mV holding potential to every command potential from −100 to +50 mV, in 1 mV increments. Before each step, every gate sits at its steady-state value for −65 mV. Because voltage is fixed during a step, each gate relaxes exponentially toward its new steady state, so no numerical integration is needed:

x(t) = x∞(V) + (x₀ − x∞(V)) · exp(−t / τ_x(V)) x∞ = α / (α + β), τ_x = 1 / (α + β)

The slider sets t, the time into the step at which current is read. The curves plot I_Na, I_K, and their sum with I_L at that moment for every command potential. Currents beyond the plotted range (−2600 to +3200 µA/cm²) are clipped at the edge of the plot.

The action potential trace

The small trace is a separate current-clamp simulation of the same model, integrated with the forward Euler method (time step 0.01 ms) for 16 ms. A 40 µA/cm² stimulus lasting 0.5 ms, starting at 1 ms, triggers the spike. The marker shows the phase each button refers to: the −20 mV crossing during the upstroke, the peak, the −20 mV crossing during repolarization, and the most negative point of the undershoot.

Matching phases to times

The time paired with each button (0, 0.3, 0.8, 2.5, and 8 ms) was chosen by hand to show what dominates during that phase. During a real action potential, voltage changes continuously and the channels never experience a clean step, so the pairing is an approximation. It works because the gating time constants are what give the action potential its shape: Na⁺ activation is fastest (well under a millisecond), Na⁺ inactivation and K⁺ activation take a few milliseconds.

What the model leaves out

  • The squid axon has only one Na⁺ and one K⁺ conductance. Mammalian neurons have many more channel types, including A-type K⁺ currents, Ca²⁺ currents, and Ca²⁺-activated K⁺ currents.
  • The model has no space: every point on the membrane is at the same voltage, as in a perfect voltage clamp.
  • Temperature is fixed at the 6.3 °C of the original experiments, so channel kinetics are several times slower than at body temperature.
