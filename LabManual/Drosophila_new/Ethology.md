# Ethological Approach to Behavior

It's possible your flies do something different that's *not* captured by a standard behavioral assay. The best way to figure this out is to watch the flies, choose a behavior to quantify, and see if those behaviors differ between your optogenetic flies and wildtype flies.

## Choosing behaviors to quantify

Your goal is to quantify **one** different behavior that your flies perform. It is up to you to define and operationalize these behaviors.

1. Place WT, Gr5a, and/or Gr66a groups of flies on ice for 2–3 minutes until they stop moving.
2. Isolate three flies of each type in separate petri dishes.

:::{admonition} Wait for the flies to wake up
:class: note
Make sure the flies are completely awake and moving before moving on to the next step. This could take about 10 minutes.
:::

3. Take a few minutes to observe both batches of flies.
4. Choose ONE different behavior that the flies do, and record it below. You also need to provide a definition for each behavior.
   A list of behaviors can be found here: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1nyXrypHe3OPKzGt07W9_UxW1nOcEfIm-/view
   And videos of the behaviors are here: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL2D0slyugueK6E9UKPqfe-fdQbiVHnlCz

5. For 5 minutes, each person in your group should observe one fly and record the number of instances those behaviors happen in the wildtype flies. Record the number of instances in the table above.
6. Do the same for the optogenetic flies. You may want to compare behavior under light and no-light conditions. 

:::{admonition} Think about your definitions
:class: tip
Could another person use your definition of each behavior and get the same count you did? What would make a behavior definition easier to apply consistently?
:::
